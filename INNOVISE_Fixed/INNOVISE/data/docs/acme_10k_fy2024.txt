Acme Corp is a manufacturer of industrial components serving original equipment customers in North America. This annual report presents our results for the fiscal years ended 2022, 2023 and 2024. Amounts are in millions of U.S. dollars.

Revenue was $80.0 million in fiscal 2022. Revenue was $90.0 million in fiscal 2023, up 12.5% from $80.0 million in fiscal 2022. Revenue was $99.9 million in fiscal 2024, up 11.0% from $90.0 million in fiscal 2023. Growth came from volume in our core product lines rather than from a change in pricing mix.

Operating income was $12.0 million in fiscal 2022. Operating income was $13.5 million in fiscal 2023, up 12.5% from $12.0 million in fiscal 2022. Operating income was $14.0 million in fiscal 2024, up 3.7% from $13.5 million in fiscal 2023. Operating costs rose with volume, and we added production capacity late in the year.

Net income was $8.0 million in fiscal 2022. Net income was $9.9 million in fiscal 2023, up 23.75% from $8.0 million in fiscal 2022. Net income was $10.4 million in fiscal 2024, up 5.05% from $9.9 million in fiscal 2023. Interest expense and a higher effective tax rate limited the gain at the bottom line.

Total debt was $30 million in fiscal 2022. Total debt was $32 million in fiscal 2023, up 6.67% from $30 million in fiscal 2022. Total debt was $38 million in fiscal 2024, up 18.75% from $32 million in fiscal 2023. Total equity was $50 million in fiscal 2022. Total equity was $54 million in fiscal 2023, up 8.0% from $50 million in fiscal 2022. Total equity was $58 million in fiscal 2024, up 7.4% from $54 million in fiscal 2023. We drew on our credit facility in fiscal 2024 to fund working capital and equipment.

Management's discussion of liquidity notes that available cash and the revolving facility were sufficient to meet obligations in fiscal 2024. This filing reports historical results for review and does not recommend any transaction.

Item 1A. Risk Factors. Our leverage and interest cost could harm results. Total debt increased in fiscal 2024, and a further rise in market rates would increase interest expense and could reduce cash available for operations. Covenants on our credit facility may also limit flexibility if earnings weaken.

Customer concentration is a material risk. A small number of OEM customers account for a large share of sales, and the loss of any major customer, or a sustained cut in their orders, would reduce revenue and operating income.

Competition in industrial components is intense. Larger rivals and low-cost importers may force price reductions or take share, which would pressure margins and could slow the revenue growth reported above.
